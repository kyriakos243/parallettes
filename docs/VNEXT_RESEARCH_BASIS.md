## Evidence Use and Limits

Research supports broad constraints; it does not prove one exact parallette progression order. Detailed node ordering combines biomechanics, established coaching practice, safe regression logic and later expert/content QA. Treat it as an architectural hypothesis to validate, not a medical claim or universal promise.

Keep three evidence classes distinct in design and copy:

1. **Direct evidence:** a cited source supports the stated relationship.
2. **Accepted coaching practice:** useful practice with limited direct experimental validation.
3. **Architectural inference:** a conservative product rule derived from evidence, mechanics and maintainability.

Do not turn correlations, apparatus-specific findings or elite-sport taxonomies into precise readiness thresholds without a versioned benchmark review.

## Research-to-Architecture Map

| Evidence | Supported architectural use | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| The 2026 ACSM resistance-training position stand supports progressive, individualised resistance training and reports that complex periodisation and training to momentary failure do not consistently improve outcomes for healthy adults: [Currier et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897). | Use simple rolling emphasis, progressive prescriptions and submaximal quality work rather than mandatory complex blocks or failure. | It does not prescribe a parallette-specific skill graph. |
| Handstand fatigue research reports degraded balance-control variability under fatigue: [García et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39334356/). | Put technical inversion, entry and balance work before high-fatigue strength. | It does not define exact session minutes or universal fatigue cut-offs. |
| A handstand biomechanics review identifies hand/wrist pressure as a central balance strategy and mixed shoulder/hip/elbow strategies when it fails: [MacDonald et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41473027/). | Model line, pressure/balance, entry, exit and weight transfer as related but distinct branches. | It does not validate one fixed consumer progression ladder. |
| Straight-arm press-to-handstand research identifies meaningful hip/compression mechanics and shoulder-loading differences between performance levels: [Mizutori et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34260617/). | Model compression, support/overhead control and press technique separately. | It does not establish a specific mobility or readiness threshold. |
| Planche modelling shows material anthropometric influence: [Wang et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37508787/). | Individualise leverage progressions; avoid time promises and one universal lever schedule. | A biomechanical model is not a validated coaching protocol. |
| Planche EMG research found different muscle-synergy patterns in preconditioning drills versus the outcome skill: [Rosaci et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40014850/). | A capacity drill or accessory is not automatically milestone evidence. | EMG similarity alone does not determine exercise value or progression order. |
| Handstand muscle activation changes across floor, parallel-bar and ring support: [Kochanowicz et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28700510/). | Record apparatus context and avoid treating evidence from different apparatus as perfectly interchangeable. | It does not require separate graphs for every apparatus variation. |
| Gymnastics wrist reviews support progressive load, technique, strength and symptom-responsive management: [Benjamin et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28902754/). | Treat upper-limb loading as current trainability plus versioned load evidence, not a permanent unlocked Boolean. | The app must not diagnose, rehabilitate or clear injuries. |
| Ratings of perceived exertion can provide accessible resistance-training intensity information: [Lea et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35000021/). | Use RPE/difficulty as one observation in dose and confidence decisions. | RPE is not a readiness oracle or capability proof. |
| The FIG Men’s Artistic Gymnastics Code identifies planche, press-handstand and related combinations as genuine high-level destinations: [FIG Code 2025–2028](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_1.1%20-%20MAG%20Code%20of%20Points%202025-2028.pdf). | Use these as an advanced-skill taxonomy and scope reference. | An elite competition code is not a consumer programming or safety guide. |

## Durable Training-Science Consequences

- Use observable benchmark facts and qualitative states, not pseudo-precise capacity or fatigue percentages.
- Historical achievement and current trainability remain separate.
- Balance, strength, mobility/access, transitions and safety use movement-type-specific confirmation policies.
- A single success does not universally unlock a skill; balance and high-risk work require consistency and safe-exit evidence.
- Technical inversion work occurs while fresh; high planche and high HSPU/press loads are not paired merely because both are unlocked.
- Recent exposure, symptoms and evidence freshness can lower today's prescription without erasing achievements.
- Anthropometry and differing mobility strategies argue for branching paths, assistance options and no universal completion timelines.
- Specialist skills require explicit opt-in, stronger evidence and conservative load compatibility; qualified coaching may be recommended.

## Product-Safety Boundary

The proposed first-release audience is healthy adults training independently. The product may screen for symptoms, stop an affected assessment branch, modify or block demands, and suggest professional help. It does not diagnose injury, prescribe rehabilitation, provide return-to-sport clearance or claim to replace qualified coaching for specialist work.


## Batch A benchmark authoring review — 2026-10-17

The exact targets are canonical in Skill Architecture and the candidate definitions. They are **authored coaching/product policy**, not validated readiness or injury thresholds. Twenty-second balance, five/eight-second holds, 60° V height, a hand-length shoulder lead, repetition counts, confirmation occasions and freshness windows distinguish observable outcomes and require repeatability; no source establishes those exact consumer thresholds. Review and version them as a package. A shorter guided demonstration is not a completed training/test occasion.

The source review retains the established evidence limits. [Yeadon & Trewartha’s hand-balance experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14999137/) supports small pressure corrections while preserving body configuration; it studied four male gymnasts and does not validate parallette-specific duration gates. [Wang & Shan’s model](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37508787/) supports treating leverage and anthropometry as relevant, not a universal Planche timeline or demographic eligibility rule. [Rosaci et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40014850/) measured different muscle recruitment in the ring support scale and preparatory exercises; this supports keeping preparation distinct from outcome evidence, with no direct validation of these parallette protocols.

The [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/) supports progressive individualised resistance training in healthy adults; it supersedes the 2009 stand consulted during the review and is not a parallette benchmark prescription. Batch A keeps short quality work, planned rest, existing symptom/freshness/load gates and earlier assisted regressions. The V target and forward-loaded push-up geometry are explicit operational definitions, not claims of proven transfer to a later Press, full Planche or specialist push-up. No new training-score, injury-clearance, equipment or population claim is introduced.

## Batch B benchmark authoring review — 2026-10-17

The exact six-/five-second Planche holds, two-second press top, two-of-up-to-four press attempts, confirmation occasions and 60-day freshness are **authored coaching/product policy**, not validated readiness, safety or injury thresholds. The [straight-arm press study of 59 male gymnasts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8279359/) supports hip-led compression, straight-arm mechanics and an unassisted grounded-start press without recoil; its participants and floor protocol do not establish consumer parallette thresholds or validate the bent-arm sibling. The [Planche model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10376746/) supports explicit lever geometry and individual differences, not proof of this exact ladder or wall-contact dose. The existing FIG taxonomy supports keeping arm/body configurations and combinations distinct; competition hold recognition is not training clearance.

Wall marks standardise a setup, not measured unloading: wall reaction and friction can supply unquantified assistance. Candidate media shows actual toe contact without upward tethers or force claims. Assisted full Planche cannot prove unassisted full Planche. The three full presses require their own complete ascents; negative, assisted, grounded-start, L-sit-start and repeated-press outcomes stay distinct. Standard practice uses one ascent per set with planned rest; the full multi-attempt test and reduced Technique practice are separate. A bounded independent content/motion review corrected scaling, contact-marker timing and practice/test wording while preserving all existing prerequisites, populations and equipment. Clinical clearance and specialist combinations remain outside Batch B.

## Batch C benchmark authoring review — 2026-10-17

[Blenkinsop et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28791131/) studied control strategies during perturbed/unperturbed handstand balance. It supports treating handstand control as a distinct motor task; it does not validate this lateral-transfer/light-hand ladder or its timings. The retained architecture separates observed contact/balance from athlete-reported easing of hand pressure; no force measurement, percentage of unloading or one-arm achievement is inferred from the drawing or ordinary feedback.

Eccentric, partial concentric, full standard and deficit outcomes are operationally distinct tests. Contraction-specific research such as [Hortobágyi et al., 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8904586/) is broader resistance-training evidence, not a validation of freestanding HSPU thresholds. The no-backcredit boundary is a conservative architectural/coaching inference: a controlled negative does not demonstrate an unobserved ascent, and a standard-range repetition does not demonstrate a deeper reviewed range. No claim is made that this strict parallette variant or a particular bailout is proven safe.

All exact attempt counts, two/three-second balance pauses, four-second negative, halfway crown marker, two-second top and confirmation/freshness windows are authored product policy. Existing wall-strength, balance and separate standard/deeper exit prerequisites remain; the exact free attempt must itself retain control, head clearance and its own feasible exit. One attempt/cycle per practice set and planned rest remain separate from multi-attempt benchmark testing. A bounded independent review corrected a knee path crossing the floor, and actual rendered-head dimensions are used for crown clearance checks. Population, apparatus, clinical-clearance and specialist boundaries are unchanged.

## Batch D benchmark authoring review — 2026-10-17

[Mizutori et al., 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0253951) examines a grounded-start pike press in trained male gymnasts. Its hip/shoulder coordination findings do not validate suspended L-sit connectors, Planche transitions or product readiness thresholds. [Prassas’s parallel-bars press analysis](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/1528) describes a stationary start, usually L-sit, to handstand; it provides movement-specific mechanical context rather than validated attempt/confirmation criteria. The four separate protocols preserve observed start configuration, complete path and direction. A grounded press, endpoint achievement or negative never substitutes for an unobserved suspended ascent.

Two accepted attempts out of at most three, two-second held endpoints, a minimum four-second negative and three occasions/45-day freshness are authored coaching/product policy, not research-validated safety or readiness cutoffs. Each scored chain retains locked elbows, straight knees and feet clearance, with ground setup and reset outside scoring. Practice is one attempt per set with planned rest; reduced endpoint holds cannot confirm. Existing prerequisites, restrictions and specialist/population/apparatus boundaries remain. Independent content/motion review corrected a thigh/forearm collision; fixed three-dimensional geometry and separation checks improve depiction without claiming measured joint load or clinical clearance.

## Batch F specialist authoring review — 2026-10-17

[Buckner et al., 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4630266/) illustrates a hands-only handstand-to-horizontal-to-handstand 90-degree push-up in a single former collegiate gymnast. Its purpose is trunk muscle/fat mapping, not validation of this adult parallette progression, safe dose or exit. The [FIG Code of Points](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_1.1%20-%20MAG%20CoP%202025-2028.pdf) distinguishes Planche/press endpoints as competition taxonomy; it is not training clearance. Two accepted attempts out of three, 2s holds, 4s lowers and three occasions/30-day freshness remain authored product/coaching rules.

Mechanical inference keeps the eccentric, complete push-up, 90-degree cycle and directional connections independently observed. The frozen handstand-through-90° “negative” is explicitly a lowering-first connection: final elbow extension into locked-arm full Planche is active and may raise shoulders. It is not purely eccentric and earns no unobserved cycle or opposite direction. Coach presence/exit conditions remain explicit; catches never count as unassisted proof. Independent source/math review and dense actual-limb geometry checks corrected a folded entry collision and misleading capacity contributions. The checks establish depiction/contract consistency, not measured forces, professional coaching approval or clinical clearance. Population, apparatus and specialist gates remain unchanged.

## Owner-approved mixed set structure — 2026-10-17

Current execution completes each exercise’s work/rest sets before advancing. Rep prescriptions are targets within timed work intervals; the timer does not count completed reps. The owner proposed rotating sets among exercises within each phase to reduce monotony. This is paired-set/round ordering and can retain rest after each exercise; it need not be a no-rest circuit.

**Direct evidence:** [Zhang et al., 2025 systematic review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02176-8) pooled 19 studies/313 participants, with only three chronic studies. Supersets shortened sessions with broadly similar pooled strength/hypertrophy outcomes but higher perceived exertion/metabolic demand; similar-biomechanics pairings reduced volume load. This does not establish equivalence for advanced parallette skill learning. [Iversen et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39072654/) found similar bench/leg-press gains but greater lat-pulldown strength gains with traditional sets, reinforcing that pairing and exercise matter. The existing [handstand fatigue study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39334356/) supports protecting fresh balance practice, not an exact rest interval or universal order.

**Programme/coaching examples:** The [r/bodyweightfitness Recommended Routine](https://www.reddit.com/r/bodyweightfitness/wiki/kb/recommended_routine/) alternates strength pairs with 90 seconds of rest after each exercise, using upper/lower or opposing movements. [GMB’s session structure](https://gmb.io/sessions/) separates preparation, fresh skill practice and simpler harder work, with circuits used in its strength example. These are useful practice examples, not experimental validation of this app’s exact session.

**Owner-approved direction:** light preparation rounds; fresh focused technique; straight sets with protected rest for demanding primary strength; optional alternating compatible support work; gentle reset flow. All-upper-limb pairings can share wrist/shoulder loading despite different exercise names. The approved implementation redistributes existing preparation/reset budgets and alternates only compatible selected support work, preserving native plan/recovery identities, rest, total duration, load gates and performed-only evidence. The exact operational contract is in Training Engine Section 6.6. Enjoyment is a product preference to test, not a proven training benefit or reason to reduce recovery.

## Workout dose investigation and approved trial — 2026-10-20/21

**Confirmed software behavior:** `scripts/investigate-vnext-workout-dose.mjs` exercises 23 synthetic goal/effort/evidence cases. All produce start-valid 25-minute plans; eight development blocks contain seven or more work sets. A separate timing-only replay of the observed 480-second Wall Shoulder-Flexion Line Drill produces eight 30-second work/30-second rest cycles, with the inherited 6–12-repetition target. The composer allocates time by block role, and execution fills that budget with cycles; it has no exercise-specific set ceiling. A low-load access/technique drill can therefore receive many sets without evidence that this is a useful dose. This is not proof that eight sets are unsafe, that they caused early finishes, or that a different dose improves outcomes.

**Evidence and limits:** the [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/) supports individualization and notes diminishing strength returns with additional sets. Its resistance-training findings do not establish an optimal dose for wall-line access, exit rehearsal or parallette skill learning. Existing fresh-skill ordering and protected rest remain appropriate boundaries; there is no new numeric fatigue/readiness rule.

Replace generic exit “reps” with an explicit practice target such as one or two controlled exits per side per set. These are authored programming proposals to validate, not research-proven cutoffs. Keep the exact four-of-up-to-six exit benchmark and all confirmation/safety/load gates unchanged. Use freed time only for independently safe, relevant selected-goal work; preserve rest. If no useful work remains, offer a transparently shorter session rather than padding. The owner explicitly approved the shorter-session policy extension on 2026-10-21. Generator 5 caps routine wall-line practice before feedback reductions, retains other selected useful work at its native dose and does not invent extra volume to refill the clock. Its versioned target reader preserves native plans/records and exact benchmarks. Operational details and QA are in Training Engine §6.19.
