## Evidence Use and Limits

Research supports broad constraints; it does not prove one exact parallette progression order. Detailed node ordering combines biomechanics, established coaching practice, safe regression logic and later expert/content QA. Treat it as an architectural hypothesis to validate, not a medical claim or universal promise.

Keep three evidence classes distinct in design and copy:

1. **Direct evidence:** a cited source supports the stated relationship.
2. **Accepted coaching practice:** useful practice with limited direct experimental validation.
3. **Architectural inference:** a conservative product rule derived from evidence, mechanics and maintainability.

Do not turn correlations, apparatus-specific findings or elite-sport taxonomies into precise readiness thresholds without a versioned benchmark review.

## Research-to-Architecture Map

| Evidence | Supported architectural use | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| The 2026 ACSM resistance-training position stand supports progressive, individualised resistance training and reports that complex periodisation and training to momentary failure do not consistently improve outcomes for healthy adults: [Currier et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897). | Use simple rolling emphasis, progressive prescriptions and submaximal quality work rather than mandatory complex blocks or failure. | It does not prescribe a parallette-specific skill graph. |
| Handstand fatigue research reports degraded balance-control variability under fatigue: [García et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39334356/). | Put technical inversion, entry and balance work before high-fatigue strength. | It does not define exact session minutes or universal fatigue cut-offs. |
| A handstand biomechanics review identifies hand/wrist pressure as a central balance strategy and mixed shoulder/hip/elbow strategies when it fails: [MacDonald et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41473027/). | Model line, pressure/balance, entry, exit and weight transfer as related but distinct branches. | It does not validate one fixed consumer progression ladder. |
| Straight-arm press-to-handstand research identifies meaningful hip/compression mechanics and shoulder-loading differences between performance levels: [Mizutori et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34260617/). | Model compression, support/overhead control and press technique separately. | It does not establish a specific mobility or readiness threshold. |
| Planche modelling shows material anthropometric influence: [Wang et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37508787/). | Individualise leverage progressions; avoid time promises and one universal lever schedule. | A biomechanical model is not a validated coaching protocol. |
| Planche EMG research found different muscle-synergy patterns in preconditioning drills versus the outcome skill: [Rosaci et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40014850/). | A capacity drill or accessory is not automatically milestone evidence. | EMG similarity alone does not determine exercise value or progression order. |
| Handstand muscle activation changes across floor, parallel-bar and ring support: [Kochanowicz et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28700510/). | Record apparatus context and avoid treating evidence from different apparatus as perfectly interchangeable. | It does not require separate graphs for every apparatus variation. |
| Gymnastics wrist reviews support progressive load, technique, strength and symptom-responsive management: [Benjamin et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28902754/). | Treat upper-limb loading as current trainability plus versioned load evidence, not a permanent unlocked Boolean. | The app must not diagnose, rehabilitate or clear injuries. |
| Ratings of perceived exertion can provide accessible resistance-training intensity information: [Lea et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35000021/). | Use RPE/difficulty as one observation in dose and confidence decisions. | RPE is not a readiness oracle or capability proof. |
| The FIG Men’s Artistic Gymnastics Code identifies planche, press-handstand and related combinations as genuine high-level destinations: [FIG Code 2025–2028](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_1.1%20-%20MAG%20Code%20of%20Points%202025-2028.pdf). | Use these as an advanced-skill taxonomy and scope reference. | An elite competition code is not a consumer programming or safety guide. |

## Durable Training-Science Consequences

- Use observable benchmark facts and qualitative states, not pseudo-precise capacity or fatigue percentages.
- Historical achievement and current trainability remain separate.
- Balance, strength, mobility/access, transitions and safety use movement-type-specific confirmation policies.
- A single success does not universally unlock a skill; balance and high-risk work require consistency and safe-exit evidence.
- Technical inversion work occurs while fresh; high planche and high HSPU/press loads are not paired merely because both are unlocked.
- Recent exposure, symptoms and evidence freshness can lower today's prescription without erasing achievements.
- Anthropometry and differing mobility strategies argue for branching paths, assistance options and no universal completion timelines.
- Specialist skills require explicit opt-in, stronger evidence and conservative load compatibility; qualified coaching may be recommended.

## Product-Safety Boundary

The proposed first-release audience is healthy adults training independently. The product may screen for symptoms, stop an affected assessment branch, modify or block demands, and suggest professional help. It does not diagnose injury, prescribe rehabilitation, provide return-to-sport clearance or claim to replace qualified coaching for specialist work.
